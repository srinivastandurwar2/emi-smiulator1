1. Aim

To study how the EMF induced in a coil depends on the magnet's strength, the number of turns and the speed of the magnet, and to verify Faraday's and Lenz's laws using a numerical model.

2. Theory

ε = −N dΦB/dt     (Faraday's law, with Lenz's minus sign)
ΦB = ∫ B·dA   →   ΦB = B A cos θ for a uniform field
Dipole on the coil axis: Φ(x) = μ0 m a² / [2 (a² + x²)3/2]
I(t) = ε(t) / R

The magnet is a point magnetic dipole of moment m = 2π rref³ B₀ / μ₀ (rref = 2 cm), so B₀ is the field on the magnet's axis 2 cm from its centre. The flux through each circular turn (radius a = √(A/π)) is computed exactly for any position and tilt using the reciprocity theorem and elliptic integrals; dΦ/dt is obtained with second-order central differences.

Conclusion

The induced EMF is proportional to the rate of change of flux linkage. For the same magnet path, the peak EMF is directly proportional to the magnet strength, the number of turns and the speed. A stationary magnet produces no EMF, and the EMF reverses sign when the motion or the polarity is reversed. The direction of the induced current always opposes the change of flux, as stated by Lenz's law.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Magnet = ideal point dipole; accurate when the magnet is small compared with its distance from the coil, approximate when it is inside or very close to the coil.
  • Thin coil: all N turns link the same flux.
  • Purely resistive circuit (I = ε/R); coil self-inductance and the magnetic force on the magnet are neglected, so the magnet's speed stays constant.
  • Instantaneous start and stop of the motion (constant-speed segments).
